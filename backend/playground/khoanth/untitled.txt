As a tenant in New York, you have several legal protections regarding heating issues in your rental unit. Here are the key laws that apply to your situation:

1. **Warranty of Habitability**  
   This is an implied warranty in all residential leases in New York, requiring landlords to maintain livable conditions. A lack of heat is a direct violation of this warranty, as it compromises the habitability of your living space.

2. **New York City Housing Maintenance Code and New York State Multiple Dwelling Law**  
   These laws specifically govern heating requirements in residential buildings. They mandate that landlords provide adequate heat from **October 1st through May 31st**. If your landlord fails to comply, they may face legal consequences, including fines and being ordered to make repairs.

3. **Constructive Eviction**  
   If the heating issue is severe and persistent, you may have grounds for claiming constructive eviction. This allows you to terminate your lease due to uninhabitable conditions.

4. **Emergency Repairs**  
   Under New York law, landlords are required to respond immediately to emergency repair requests, especially for issues affecting health or safety, such as heating failures during winter.

5. **Withholding Rent**  
   If your landlord does not address the heating issue within a reasonable time after being notified, you may have the right to withhold rent. It is advisable to inform your landlord in writing of the repairs needed and your intention to withhold rent if the issue is not resolved.

6. **Documentation**  
   It is crucial to document the heating issue. Take pictures, keep records of communication with your landlord, and note the dates when the heating was not functioning.
